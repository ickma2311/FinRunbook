# Company analysis

Define the decision: operating performance, financial resilience, principal
risks or investment attractiveness. Identify the issuer, listed security and
businesses. Explain how revenue is earned, which costs scale with it, and who
supplies capital. Private-company disclosures may require narrower conclusions.

For a substantial public-company report, build five fiscal years and the latest
interim with matched prior-year interim figures. Include revenue, gross profit,
operating income, net income, operating cash flow, cash capital spending, cash,
debt, receivables and inventory where relevant. Define FCF; show acquisitions,
investments and financing separately. Add segment revenue and business-specific
KPIs. Do not substitute a growth-only narrative for comparable financial data.

Separate demand, share, price/mix, capacity and accounting effects. Explain
competitive advantage through economics, switching costs, distribution or
customer behavior; high margins alone do not establish persistence. Compare
consistent scopes; segment profit is not necessarily fully allocated profit.

Prioritize risks by exposure, transmission mechanism and monitoring horizon.
For each material risk show evidence, affected revenue/cost/capital, offsets
and a condition that would strengthen or weaken the view. Quantify concentration,
maturity or sensitivity when supported. Commitments, debt, guarantees and
expected losses are different. Separate observed events from possible risks.

Include a reconciled profit/cash bridge or transparent sensitivity. State fixed
inputs and omitted effects. Retain contrary evidence. Do not assign unsupported
probabilities, identify anonymous customers, infer causal catalysts from
correlation, or add a price target to a purely operating-risk question.

Deliver the analyst view, decisive figures, financial history, principal risks,
forward indicators and accessible source-linked limitations. A brief question
needs only the relevant finding and evidence, not every report panel above.
