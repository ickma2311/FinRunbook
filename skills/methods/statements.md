# Financial statements

Normalize income, balance and cash-flow statements before ratios. Retain fiscal
start/end, annual/interim, duration/instant, currency, scale, sign and accounting
basis. Use appropriate restated comparatives while preserving original conflicts.
Missing figures are not zero; adjacent scopes are not silent substitutes.

Profitability: revenue growth, gross/operating/net margins and their drivers.
Separate acquisitions, FX, restructuring, investment gains, taxes and discontinued
operations. Investment or tax gains do not demonstrate better operations.

Cash conversion: compare CFO and net income over matching periods. Reconcile
noncash adjustments and operating asset/liability changes. Define cash capex,
including treatment of financing principal, software, intangibles and leases.
FCF equals CFO less the stated measure; label proxies. Show acquisitions,
equity investments, repurchases, dividends and debt proceeds separately.

Working capital: receivables, inventory, payables, contract assets, deferred
revenue and customer advances. DSO = average receivables / credit revenue ×
period days; inventory days = average inventory / cost of sales × days. Missing
credit revenue/opening balances require labeled proxies. Ending balances are
not averages. Growth, mix and seasonality can move ratios without misconduct.

Liquidity and solvency: cash quality, restrictions, debt carrying/face values,
maturities, rates, covenants and refinancing. Current ratio = current assets /
current liabilities; volatile equities or inventory can limit its usefulness.
Define net debt and EBITDA exclusions. Do not treat every investment as available
cash. ROIC requires stated NOPAT and average invested-capital definitions.

Review recognition policies, capitalization, provisions, related parties,
contingencies and subsequent events. An anomaly identifies a question, not
fraud. Contractual exposure differs from probable loss. Reconciled arithmetic
does not prove economic quality or remove accounting-estimate uncertainty.
