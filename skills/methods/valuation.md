# Valuation and expectations

Identify security, currency, valuation date, price session, diluted shares,
cash/debt dates and forecast vintage. Missing market price permits a hypothetical
price sensitivity or operating analysis, not a claim about current upside or
an observed valuation multiple.

Choose business-appropriate multiples, DCF, sum of parts or asset-value methods.
Explain peer selection and scope differences. Match equity value with equity
earnings/cash flow and enterprise value with operating metrics. EV starts with
market capitalization plus debt, preferred stock and noncontrolling interests
less available cash; document leases, investments and other adjustments. Keep
TTM actuals separate from fiscal forecasts. Negative denominators need separate
treatment, not misleading rankings.

DCF needs explicit growth, margin, tax, reinvestment and working-capital drivers.
Use WACC for unlevered cash flows, cost of equity for equity cash flows. State
horizon, discount convention and terminal assumptions; terminal growth must
remain below the discount rate. Show terminal-value dependence. Stock-based
compensation has an economic cost: account for dilution or replacement cash
spending rather than ignoring both.

Reconcile enterprise to equity and per-share value. Scenarios expose assumptions;
precision in arithmetic is not precision in forecasts. Reverse valuation asks
what operating path the actual price implies and tests it against capacity,
history and competition. Keep analyst assumptions, issuer guidance and consensus
distinct. Add a recommendation only when requested and supported by price and
risk evidence.
